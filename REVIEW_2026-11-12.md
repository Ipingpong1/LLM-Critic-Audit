Reviewed 2026-11-12 against the AAAI-27 workshop *Effective and Efficient Evaluation for AI Scientists* (deadline 20 Nov 2026, 06:00 UTC; full papers 6–8 pages, references excluded; non-archival; double-blind; in-person presentation required; attendance by invitation, capped at 45–65).

What was checked: README, RESEARCH_HISTORY, all six plans in `prereg/`, the manuscript source and figures, `pilot/analyze.py`, `pilot/prompts.py`, the 2026-11-12 results freeze (`results/freeze_2026-11-12/`), the label-sensitivity report, the audit state, the bibliography, the novelty report, and the workshop and AAAI policy pages. `scripts/release_data.py verify` passes (97 files), `make_figures.py --only numbers` reproduces every number in the paper from saved replies, the 21 unit tests pass, and the manuscript compiles to 14 pages with pdfLaTeX. Seven central references (Brown et al. 2611.05662, Mazaheri 2608.16003, Dong 2610.15415, SoundnessBench 2605.30329, Quiroz on Zenodo, Lijo 2610.35942, Yang 2607.01240) were confirmed to exist with matching titles and authors.

## Verdict

**Not too weak. Submit, but not as it stands.** By the standard of a non-archival workshop paper this is unusually well-powered and well-controlled: about 224,000 replies, 14 configurations from three families plus two models of a fourth, preregistered claim tests with 16 of 18 contrasts holding at Holm p ≤ .002, a held-out replication, a label-sensitivity bound, and a consistent result across six wordings of the rate. The question, the signal-detection framing, and the workshop's stated topics (LLM-as-judge reliability, calibration, adversarial robustness, cost-normalized reporting, critiques of evaluation practice) line up directly. The ideal-observer scale for a critic's threshold is still unclaimed in the literature as of the 10 Nov search.

The risk is not weakness. It is four concrete problems that would be caught by a careful reviewer or by the venue, and one scientific soft spot. Each is fixable in the eight days left.

### 1. The manuscript disagrees with the project's own corrected analysis for GPT-6.1 Sol

`results/freeze_2026-11-12/` reran the claim contrasts with an exact within-item randomization test (the appropriate test for paired binary verdicts) instead of bootstrap p-values. The 18 original contrasts are unchanged: 16 hold, with far smaller p-values than before. **For GPT-6.1 Sol, only 4 of 9 hold (T2, T3, T8, T9), not the 7 of 9 that Table 4, Section 4.3 ("passed seven of the nine contrasts") and Appendix E state.** T1, T5 and T6 are each a shift of −0.09, meaning six verdicts out of 200 moved one way; an exact sign test gives p = .031 uncorrected and p = .156 after Holm over nine tests. The bootstrap p of .003 for a six-verdict change was anti-conservative.

What to change: Table 4 marks for T1, T5, T6 become "not shown"; the sentence in 4.3 becomes "four of nine"; the framing "the claim effect is in a fourth family" is still supportable from T2/T3/T8/T9, but say plainly that for this critic only the flat assertion moved it, and the original wording with "Check it carefully." did not reliably. The abstract's "27 to 100" (T2) stands.

### 2. The wording follow-up overstates what differed

Section 4.5 and Appendix E say three of 60 wording comparisons differed. The freeze's studentized problem-cluster bootstrap (validated at the nominal 5% level in simulation) finds one: Qwen3.5-4B without reasoning, audit wording. The Qwen3.5-4B "user message" and Qwen3.5-27B "audit" differences do not survive. The "27 showed no difference within ±0.15" statement is an equivalence claim; the freeze declines to carry it forward. Report one difference, and describe the rest as "no difference detected" with the interval bounds, not as equivalence.

### 3. The pending label audit and the ceiling bias

A `\pending{}` marker is still in Section 4.5. Nobody has judged the 42-item blind sheet. This audit decides whether ρ for reasoning-on frontier critics is about 0.2 (original labels) or about 0.5 (contested items dropped or flipped). It must be done by a human who has not seen the critics' objections (`prereg/label_sensitivity.md` records that the AI assistant has), so this is a task for you, blind, from `audit/START_HERE.html`, several hours.

Separately, `validation.json` in the freeze shows that near ceiling ρ is biased toward zero: at d′ = 4.5 with 200 items, a true ρ of 0.5 is estimated at 0.35 on average and the 95% interval covers the truth 80% of the time. At the reasoning-on critics' actual d′ (3.1–3.3) coverage is 91% and bias is small, so the original-label numbers are fine. But the bias runs in the paper's favour under the drop/flip variants, and the paper only says "measured near ceiling". State the direction of the bias in Section 4.5 and in Limitations whatever the audit shows.

If the audit cannot be done by the 20th: remove the marker, keep the bounds, and say in Limitations that the audit is pending. Do not submit with a `\pending{}` in the PDF.

## Scientific weaknesses to address in the text

- **The normative premise.** A critic sees one solution, cannot verify the stated rate, and the stated rate is never the true rate. Discounting it could be rational, and a model trained to resist system-prompt steering of per-item verdicts is behaving as its developers intended. The paper's rebuttal (the instruction did not help; critics did act on the "random" note) is partial. Two existing results answer this better than the current text does, and both deserve promotion from the appendix to the Results: the own-confidence benchmark (a Bayesian using the critic's *own* stated probabilities would change 29–44 verdicts; the critics change 3–14), which does not depend on whether the rate is true, and the "accuracy if the rate were true" calculation (55–91% against 91–98% for an ideal observer at a 10% rate), which is the practical cost in one line.
- **The "cheap remedy" overclaims.** The Discussion says "one sentence about the note's reliability neutralized it" and "stating a note's reliability is a cheap remedy". The random-note line changes two things at once: the claim becomes *a note attached to the solution* instead of the system prompt's own assertion, and it is declared uninformative. The freeze flags this: "attribution and declared randomness are confounded". A control "A note attached to this solution says it contains an error." (attribution, no reliability statement) would isolate it; for Opus that is about 200 calls and a few dollars, for Qwen3.5-27B a few hours on the cluster. Run it if there is time; otherwise soften both sentences to "consistent with discounting".
- **Effective probability is a shift-equivalent, not a belief.** "Opus ... treats [the claim] like a stated rate of 99%" reads as an inferred internal belief. Keep the language at "moved as far as an ideal observer told 99% would".
- **Three reasoning-on headlines are shakier than the thinking-off ones.** "Reasoning improves detection, not the use of the rate" rests on critics at or near ceiling with label-dependent ρ. The thinking-off, OpenAI and Haiku results are firm under every label reading; say explicitly which findings are, and which await the audit. The GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna ρ results (never at ceiling, intervals below 0.43 under every reading) are the cleanest evidence for reasoning-on critics and deserve a sentence in Section 4.1, not only Appendix E.
- **The post hoc threshold result can be upgraded.** The freeze's cross-validated threshold analysis (thresholds fit on neutral replies, applied under the bare claim, problem-level folds) gives Opus hit 0.92 and false alarms 0.05, confirming the in-sample "cut at 80" illustration. Report the cross-validated version; it answers the overfitting objection. For Qwen3.5-27B and Sol the fitted policy at 10% prevalence degenerates to "flag nothing", which should be said when claiming no cut-off recovers them.
- **Nine hard-set problems have two solutions** (five with both labels). The item bootstrap treats them as independent; the freeze resamples whole problems and the intervals change by at most 0.01. Mention it in Method, or adopt the freeze's intervals.
- **"Preregistered" without a registry.** Plans were timestamped by commits in a private repository. Reviewers vary on this. "Pre-specified, with plans fixed and dated before data collection (supplement)" is the safer word, and the dated commit log in `.local/provenance/source-history.txt` should go into the supplement with the code.
- **Sol's T7 "no shift" rests on a zero-width interval** (all 200 verdicts identical). Say "no verdict changed" rather than claiming an interval within ±0.3.
