Example 1
Problem: In an increasing sequence of numbers with an odd number of terms, the difference between any two consecutive terms is a constant $d$, and the middle term is 302 . When the last 4 terms are removed from the sequence, the middle term of the resulting sequence is 296. What is the value of $d$ ?
Solution:
Step 1: Let's denote the sequence as \(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_{2n+1}\), where \(a_i = a_1 + (i-1)d\). Since the sequence has an odd number of terms, the middle term is \(a_{n+1} = 302\). This means: \[a_{n+1} = a_1 + nd = 302.\]
Step 2: When the last 4 terms are removed, the new sequence is \(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_{2n-3}\) and the middle term of this new sequence is \(a_n = 296\). This means: \[a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d = 296.\]
Step 3: We now have two equations:
First, \(a_1 + nd = 302\)
Second, \(a_1 + (n-1)d = 296\)
Step 4: Subtracting the second equation from the first gives: \[(a_1 + nd) - (a_1 + (n-1)d) = 302 - 296\] \[nd - (n-1)d = 6\] \[nd - nd + d = 6\] \[d = 6.\]
Step 5: Thus, the value of \(d\) is \(\boxed{6}\).
Audited verdict: error

Notice that for the fraction to equal \(-\frac{1}{2}\), the numerator must be negative twice the magnitude of the denominator because the only way to achieve a fraction that is negative and has a denominator that is always positive (since squares are non-negative) is if the numerator is negative and its absolute value is twice the denominator.
Step 2: Let's denote \(A = x - 2013\) and \(B = y - 2014\), so our equation becomes: \[ \frac{AB}{A^2 + B^2} = -\frac{1}{2}. \] Multiplying both sides by \(2(A^2 + B^2)\) gives: \[ 2AB = -(A^2 + B^2). \] Rearranging terms: \[ A^2 + 2AB + B^2 = 0. \]

Step 5: Therefore, the value of \(x + y\) is \(4027\). **Final Answer:** \(x + y = 4027\).

Step 3: Now, substitute this back into the original expression: $x^{2}+2x-x(x+1) = x^{2} + 2x - (x^{2} + x)$

Step 5: Simplify the expression further by combining the $x$ terms: $x + (-x) = 0$
Step 6: So, when $x=2018$, the value of the expression is $\boxed{0}$.

Step 2: Next, simplify the exponent inside the radical. We have $9^2 - 9 = 81 - 9 = 72$. So, the expression is now $\sqrt{\sqrt{\sqrt[9]{10^{72}}}}$.
Step 3: Now, deal with the innermost radical (the 9th root). The 9th root of $10^{72}$ can be simplified because $10^{72} = (10^{8})^{9}$, so $\sqrt[9]{10^{72}} = 10^{8}$.
Step 4: Next, handle the next radical (square root). Now we have $\sqrt{\sqrt{10^{8}}}$. Since $\sqrt{10^{8}} = 10^{8/2} = 10^{4}$, the expression simplifies to $\sqrt{10^{4}}$.
Step 5: Finally, take the square root of $10^{4}$. This gives us $10^{4/2} = 10^{2} = 100$. Therefore, $\sqrt{\sqrt{\sqrt[9]{10^{9^{2}-9}}}} = \boxed{100}$. This completes the computation.

Step 2: Second, determine the number of yellow roses needed for \(\frac{2}{7}\) of the roses to be yellow. Let \( y \) be the number of yellow roses left after some are removed. We want: \[ \frac{y}{49 - x} = \frac{2}{7} \] where \( x \) is the number of yellow roses removed. However, since we are only removing yellow roses, \( x \) is not directly needed. Instead, we use: \[ y = \frac{2}{7} (49) \] Calculate: \[ y = \frac{2 \times 49}{7} = \frac{98}{7} = 14 \] So, there should be 14 yellow roses left.
Step 3: Third, determine how many yellow roses need to be removed. Initially, there are 19 yellow roses. To find out how many need to be removed, subtract the number of yellow roses left from the initial number: \[ 19 - 14 = 5 \]
Step 4: Therefore, Rad needs to remove \(\boxed{5}\) yellow roses.

Solution:
Step 1: To solve this problem, we can use the fact that the list \( p, q, r, s \) consists of four consecutive integers listed in increasing order. First, let's denote the first integer as \( p \). Since the integers are consecutive, we can express the other three integers in terms of \( p \): \( p, p+1, p+2, p+3 \). Since \( s \) is the last integer in the list, we know that \( s = p + 3 \).
Step 2: We are given that \( p + s = 109 \). Substituting \( s = p + 3 \) into this equation, we get: \( p + (p + 3) = 109 \). Combine like terms: \( 2p + 3 = 109 \). Subtract 3 from both sides: \( 2p = 106 \). Divide both sides by 2: \( p = 53 \).
Step 3: Now that we have found \( p \), we can find the values of \( q \) and \( r \): \( q = p + 1 = 53 + 1 = 54 \) and \( r = p + 2 = 53 + 2 = 55 \).
Step 4: Finally, we need to find the sum of \( q \) and \( r \): \( q + r = 54 + 55 = 109 \). However, looking back at our original equations, we realize that \( q + r \) must be one less than \( p + s \), since \( q \) and \( r \) are the middle two numbers in the sequence. Therefore, we can conclude that: \( \boxed{q + r = 108} \).
